Chamomile Botanical Guide

Calm, Care, and Tradition

Origin & Habitat

Chamomile species are native to Europe and Western Asia and are now cultivated widely in temperate regions. They thrive in open, sunny locations with well-drained soils and are commonly found in meadows, field margins, and herb gardens.

Varieties

German chamomile (Matricaria chamomilla): annual, aromatic, widely used for infusions. Roman chamomile (Chamaemelum nobile): low-growing perennial with apple-like scent, used in gardens and aromatic preparations.

Classic Records

Chamomile has a long history in European and Mediterranean herbal traditions, valued for its gentle, soothing character. It appears in historical herbals and household remedies as a calming infusion and topical preparation.

Brewing Methods

Infusion: 1–2 teaspoons dried flowers per cup (200–250 ml) of hot water; cover and steep 5–10 minutes, then strain. Stronger preparation: increase flower quantity or steep longer for aromatic compresses (external use).

Dosage & Cautions

Commonly enjoyed as tea in moderate amounts. People allergic to Asteraceae plants (e.g. ragweed, daisies, chrysanthemums, marigolds) may react. Use caution during pregnancy or breastfeeding, with anticoagulant medicines (such as warfarin), sedatives, or before surgery; consult a qualified health professional when unsure.

Products & Processing

Flowers are harvested at bloom, dried carefully to preserve volatile oils (including azulenes in some preparations), and processed into loose tea, tea bags, extracts, essential oils, hydrosols, and skincare ingredients.

The Design Rule — Read Before Day 16

Main app UI = black and white. Like a lawyer's uniform. Background #0A0A0A, text #FAFAF8, borders rgba(250,248,240,0.08), single gold accent #C9A84C only. No blue, no green, no purple, no red anywhere in the main UI. Severity is expressed through brightness (dim=low risk, bright=high risk) — never hue. Fraunces serif for headings. DM Mono for labels. Barlow for body. The ONLY exception is the Lex orb page — that's the user's personal visual portal in whatever colour they chose. Every Claude session for Days 16–25 and 41–45: remind Claude "design is strictly black and white, lawyer's uniform, single gold accent only."

The Orb Speaks Back — How It Works

When Lex speaks (TTS), the orb visually reacts in real time in the user's chosen colour. useLexTTS generates a simulated amplitude curve during speech → fires onAmplitude(0–1) every 50ms → LexPage passes amplitude to LexOrb → orb core brightness, ring glow, and scan line speed all scale with the amplitude value. The orb breathes and pulses as Lex talks. When silent, it returns to idle pulse. The colour (violet/cyan/gold/ember/arctic/jade/rose/solar) is whatever the user picked during onboarding — stored in DB, loaded from useAuth().orbColor.

How to use PlantGuard efficiently.

1. Diagnose conditions

Open Plant Diagnosis, set soil moisture, temperature, light, and humidity, then run the neural assessment. Review status, confidence, and prioritized recommendations.

2. Check weather context

Open Weather Insights to fetch local conditions and receive practical guidance (e.g. rainfall, heat, wind, humidity considerations).

3. Use the knowledge network

Open Encyclopedia to search and filter across crops, weeds, and special plants. Expand entries for lifespan, vulnerabilities/weaknesses, management or control, and impact.

4. Ask the Assistant

Open Assistant to ask questions in natural language. It can explain your latest diagnosis, interpret weather context, and retrieve details from the knowledge network (plants, weeds, vulnerabilities, management, and more).

The diagnosis summary can be read aloud via your browser’s speech synthesis (where supported). Weather and location features require permission and connectivity.
